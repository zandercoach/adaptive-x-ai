# Learning Resources

The learning on this journey doesn't only happen in sessions. Books, videos,
webinars, articles and trainings feed into it from the outside. This file is the
source of truth for that input; `docs/resources.html` is its published rendering.

It is a **complete log, not a recommendation list** — things that turned out to be
a waste of time stay in, labelled as such. That is the more useful signal, and it
fits a journal that publishes its mistakes.

## What an entry contains

Two layers, deliberately separated — the same division of labour the journal uses
(see `CLAUDE.md`): the facts may be drafted by an agent, the take is handwritten.

| Field | Who | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Title, author, format, link | agent | Format: book, video, webinar, article, training, podcast, course |
| Consumed | agent/Christian | Date or period |
| Source material | agent | Where the material sits and what its licence allows. A public link where there is one; otherwise say plainly that it is not public and why, so the missing link is not a mystery |
| What it argues | agent | 2–3 sentences, factual, no evaluation |
| What I took from it | **Christian** | The point of the whole page |
| Where it landed | **Christian** | Which journal entry, decision or habit it actually influenced |
| Verdict | **Christian** | Worth someone else's time? For whom? |

Rules of the house:

- Never write "What I took from it", "Where it landed" or "Verdict" from
  inference. Ask Christian, one question at a time. Write his answers close to
  his own words, as self-contained statements.
- Link, characterize and comment — do not reproduce the content of a source. This
  holds even where a licence would allow more: the value of an entry is the take,
  not the material.
- Material that is not ours to publish stays outside the repo entirely. Name it,
  say where it sits, move on.
- An entry with only the factual layer filled in is fine and stays visibly
  incomplete. Half an entry is honest; an invented take is not.

## Themes

Grouped by the three models from the working hypothesis, plus the craft layer:

- **Business model** — how value is created and sold when AI changes the economics.
- **Leadership model** — leading people and agents; judgement, trust, accountability.
- **Operating model** — how work is organized, teams are structured, decisions flow.
- **Craft** — agentic engineering, tools, hands-on practice.

A source can sit in more than one. If nothing fits, add a theme rather than
forcing it.

### "AI for Leaders" (Level 1) — Agile Academy

| | |
|---|---|
| **Facilitator** | Sohrab Salimi, Founder & CEO of Agile Academy — a fellow trainer and a mentor of mine |
| **Format** | Training. Normally two days, in person or remote; for the ambassador cohort it ran as four afternoons |
| **Consumed** | 21–22 and 28–29 July 2026 |
| **Context** | Attended as one of the Agile Academy's ambassadors |
| **Themes** | Leadership model · Business model · Operating model |
| **Source material** | The deck is licensed CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 — free to share, but not for public republication, which is what this repo would be. Not reproduced here; characterized only |

**What it argues.** The premise is that AI is infrastructure, not software: the
ground the business runs on, touching People, Process and Product at once — which
makes it a leadership question rather than an IT purchase, and explains why so
few AI initiatives return anything. Its central move is the *Player Coach*: as
the cost of delivering work collapses and the value-creating unit shrinks toward
one person plus their agents, the coordinating manager role loses its reason to
exist and leaders are pulled back into the work itself. The course design follows
its own argument — leaders build an agent with their own hands on day one before
being asked to lead anything on day two.

**What I took from it.**

- Confirmed: AI is infrastructure, not IT. It has an effect on virtually every
  aspect and every step of the value chain of products, it can open new markets
  for new business models, and it reaches the people in the organization and how
  they work together — including with AI — and therefore how people and companies
  are led.
- Confirmed: the delivery part of value creation gets much faster and more
  compact, and becomes possible with smaller teams, all the way down to "1 + AI"
  teams.
- Confirmed intuition: leadership moves back close to value creation, as far as
  actively shaping it — the Player Coach.
- Beyond confirmation: the Player Coach makes explicit what I had only thought
  intuitively — and why I am on this journey at all.
- New as an explicit point: companies *and* leaders should not outsource the
  topic of AI, because it has to become part of the DNA of the core business.
- New as a decision: how deeply to take AI into your own hands is an important
  choice — "only" the application layer, or the model layer, or even the
  infrastructure layer. In other words, what degree of sovereignty.

**Where it landed.**

- Morten as an agent on Abundly, and Morten as part of a team. (The training runs
  on Abundly too — Agile Academy is their first worldwide partner.)
- Morten as part of *one* team — no dedicated marketing team any more, only a
  core team. "1 + AI" for the moment, maybe "2 + AI" later. **Done on
  2026-07-31:** the GitHub team `marketing` became `crew`, Morten's role in it
  is Marketing & Sales, and the Slack channels were split into `#crew` for the
  normal case and `#crew-alerts` for escalation. Three days from the training
  insight to the org chart.
- The move to Player Coach was inspired here as well. It is datable: the line in
  `team/christian-zander.md` about being "back in the game as a player-coach"
  went in on 2026-07-28 — the same afternoon the training covered the model, and
  before the source was named anywhere (see
  `research/20260728-company-brain-and-the-team.md`).
- I will check the learning journey for business-model viability — moonshot — the
  same way I check my existing business and the repositioning. And probably the
  tool we are learning on as well: the Chronicler.

**Verdict.** Absolutely worth recommending for leaders who are ready to question
an existing understanding of leadership and an existing understanding of
organization.
