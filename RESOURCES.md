# Learning Resources

The learning on this journey doesn't only happen in sessions. Books, videos,
webinars, articles and trainings feed into it from the outside. This file is the
source of truth for that input; `docs/resources.html` is its published rendering.

It is a **complete log, not a recommendation list** — things that turned out to be
a waste of time stay in, labelled as such. That is the more useful signal, and it
fits a journal that publishes its mistakes.

## What an entry contains

Two layers, deliberately separated — the same division of labour the journal uses
(see `CLAUDE.md`): the facts may be drafted by an agent, the take is handwritten.

| Field | Who | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Title, author, format, link | agent | Format: book, video, webinar, article, training, podcast, course |
| Consumed | agent/Christian | Date or period |
| Source material | agent | Where the material sits and what its licence allows. A public link where there is one; otherwise say plainly that it is not public and why, so the missing link is not a mystery |
| What it argues | agent | 2–3 sentences, factual, no evaluation |
| What I took from it | **Christian** | The point of the whole page |
| Where it landed | **Christian** | Which journal entry, decision or habit it actually influenced |
| Verdict | **Christian** | Worth someone else's time? For whom? |

Rules of the house:

- Never write "What I took from it", "Where it landed" or "Verdict" from
  inference. Ask Christian, one question at a time. Write his answers close to
  his own words, as self-contained statements.
- Link, characterise and comment — do not reproduce the content of a source. This
  holds even where a licence would allow more: the value of an entry is the take,
  not the material.
- Material that is not ours to publish stays outside the repo entirely. Name it,
  say where it sits, move on.
- An entry with only the factual layer filled in is fine and stays visibly
  incomplete. Half an entry is honest; an invented take is not.

## Themes

Grouped by the three models from the working hypothesis, plus the craft layer:

- **Business model** — how value is created and sold when AI changes the economics.
- **Leadership model** — leading people and agents; judgement, trust, accountability.
- **Operating model** — how work is organized, teams are structured, decisions flow.
- **Craft** — agentic engineering, tools, hands-on practice.

A source can sit in more than one. If nothing fits, add a theme rather than
forcing it.

### "Agentic Coding Teams" — Pierluigi Pugliese, Dario Zanotto

| | |
|---|---|
| **Authors** | Pierluigi Pugliese and Dario Zanotto, Connexxo. German edition by Dr. Jürgen Hoffmann |
| **Format** | Book, PDF, 103 pages. Connexxo GmbH, 2026; published in English, German and Italian — <https://connexxo.com/resources/agentic-coding-teams/> |
| **Consumed** | August 2026, the German edition |
| **Themes** | Craft · Operating model · Leadership model |
| **Source material** | Bought through the Connexxo shop. Each PDF is personalised to its buyer, which is one more reason it is not reproduced here; characterised only |

**What it argues.** An evidence-based account of how teams of humans actually
work with AI agents, positioned against both marketing poles — the claim that
nothing essential has changed, and the promise that human teams are about to
disappear. It runs in six parts: what the evidence supports on team size and on
the patterns of human-agent collaboration; how teamwork, developer skills and the
path from junior to senior change; the technical disciplines that keep agents on
course, from the XP practices through domain-driven design to shared standards
and the platform; the hidden costs — cognitive load, cognitive fatigue, token
spend, security; what changes for Scrum Masters and Agile Coaches; and finally
governance, sovereignty and the gap between hype and reality. The book was itself
drafted largely by AI, with every substantive claim checked against its source —
a method it states up front and documents in a closing note.

**What I took from it.**

- The role perspective, and the discussion of team size and of AI's impact on
  cross-functional (agile) teams.

**Where it landed.**

- Nothing concrete yet. It sharpened my understanding.

**Verdict.** Highly recommended for anyone working with agile teams who is
looking for solid evidence behind claims about how those teams should be composed
and how they should work in relation to AI.

### "AI for Leaders" (Level 1) — Agile Academy

| | |
|---|---|
| **Facilitator** | Sohrab Salimi, Founder & CEO of Agile Academy — a fellow trainer and a mentor of mine |
| **Format** | Training. Normally two days, in person or remote; for the ambassador cohort it ran as four afternoons |
| **Consumed** | 21–22 and 28–29 July 2026 |
| **Context** | Attended as one of the Agile Academy's ambassadors |
| **Themes** | Leadership model · Business model · Operating model |
| **Source material** | The deck is licensed CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 — free to share, but not for public republication, which is what this repo would be. Not reproduced here; characterised only |

**What it argues.** The premise is that AI is infrastructure, not software: the
ground the business runs on, touching People, Process and Product at once — which
makes it a leadership question rather than an IT purchase, and explains why so
few AI initiatives return anything. Its central move is the *Player Coach*: as
the cost of delivering work collapses and the value-creating unit shrinks toward
one person plus their agents, the coordinating manager role loses its reason to
exist and leaders are pulled back into the work itself. The course design follows
its own argument — leaders build an agent with their own hands on day one before
being asked to lead anything on day two.

**What I took from it.**

- Confirmed: AI is infrastructure, not IT. It has an effect on virtually every
  aspect and every step of the value chain of products, it can open new markets
  for new business models, and it reaches the people in the organisation and how
  they work together — including with AI — and therefore how people and companies
  are led.
- Confirmed: the delivery part of value creation gets much faster and more
  compact, and becomes possible with smaller teams, all the way down to "1 + AI"
  teams.
- Confirmed intuition: leadership moves back close to value creation, as far as
  actively shaping it — the Player Coach.
- Beyond confirmation: the Player Coach makes explicit what I had only thought
  intuitively — and why I am on this journey at all.
- New as an explicit point: companies *and* leaders should not outsource the
  topic of AI, because it has to become part of the DNA of the core business.
- New as a decision: how deeply to take AI into your own hands is an important
  choice — "only" the application layer, or the model layer, or even the
  infrastructure layer. In other words, what degree of sovereignty.

**Where it landed.**

- Morten as an agent on Abundly, and Morten as part of a team. (The training runs
  on Abundly too — Agile Academy is their first worldwide partner.)
- Morten as part of *one* team — no dedicated marketing team any more, only a
  core team. "1 + AI" for the moment, maybe "2 + AI" later. **Done on
  2026-07-31:** the GitHub team `marketing` became `crew`, Morten's role in it
  is Marketing & Sales, and the Slack channels were split into `#crew` for the
  normal case and `#crew-alerts` for escalation. Three days from the training
  insight to the org chart.
- The move to Player Coach was inspired here as well. It is datable: the line in
  `team/christian-zander.md` about being "back in the game as a player-coach"
  went in on 2026-07-28 — the same afternoon the training covered the model, and
  before the source was named anywhere (see
  `research/20260728-company-brain-and-the-team.md`).
- I will check the learning journey for business-model viability — moonshot — the
  same way I check my existing business and the repositioning. And probably the
  tool we are learning on as well: the Chronicler.

**Verdict.** Absolutely worth recommending for leaders who are ready to question
an existing understanding of leadership and an existing understanding of
organisation.

### "From agent use cases to agentic organization" — Nils Janse / Abundly

| | |
|---|---|
| **Speaker** | Nils Janse, Co-founder & Gen AI Expert at Abundly AI |
| **Format** | Webinar, online, 59 minutes — <https://www.abundly.ai/events/2026-06-23/webinar-from-agent-use-cases-to-agentic-organization> |
| **Consumed** | 23 June 2026, live |
| **Themes** | Operating model · Leadership model |
| **Source material** | The event page carries the description and the recording. Linked and characterised here, not reproduced |

**What it argues.** The step that matters is not the single agent but the fleet:
how agents across business functions are wired into end-to-end processes rather
than left standing as isolated tools. It offers a repeatable way of getting there
for any function — a method for turning scattered agent ideas into a structured
portfolio — and shows concrete fleet examples. It takes an organisation that is
already building agents as its starting point, and addresses executives and
department heads, AI leads structuring an agent portfolio, and the champions and
agent builders who want to see how their own agents fit the bigger picture.

**What I took from it.**

- How demanding the road to a genuinely agentic organisation is.

**Where it landed.**

- On the basis of this webinar I wanted to build the learning journey
  professionally, and to do everything that happens in a way that stays clean and
  maintainable even if I scale up to several agents.

**Verdict.** Worth recommending for people with experience — at minimum with AI
assistants, ideally with agentic engineering as well.

### "The secure road to AI agents — Governance & Guardrails" — Nils Janse / Abundly

| | |
|---|---|
| **Speaker** | Nils Janse, Co-founder & Gen AI Expert at Abundly AI |
| **Format** | Webinar, online, one hour — <https://www.abundly.ai/events/2026-05-28/webinar-the-secure-road-to-ai-agents-governance-guardrails> |
| **Consumed** | 20 June 2026, as a recording; the session itself ran on 28 May 2026 |
| **Themes** | Operating model · Leadership model |
| **Source material** | The event page carries the description and the recording. Linked and characterised here, not reproduced |

**What it argues.** Governance is what makes agents scalable rather than what
holds them back: who is allowed to create an agent, how agents get reviewed, what
data and systems they may reach, how their performance is monitored, and who
carries the risk. It works through the conversations to have with IT, the
guardrails that let people build fast without giving up safety, and the ownership
structures a growing agent portfolio needs. Addressed at AI leads, at the C-level
accountable for direction, ROI, risk and security, at heads of IT and compliance,
at engineering and platform leads, and at the department heads whose teams build
and use agents.

**What I took from it.**

- What matters to me is the triad of structure, alignment and ownership — and a
  governance model that sets the right frame at the right phase.
- I like the "Foundation – Factory – Fleet" steps as a way of growing
  organisations into agentic organisations.

**Where it landed.**

- I worked in a considerably more organised and structured way afterwards:
  Morten's permissions, the PR gates, the separation of the repos.
- All of that is still the "Foundation" phase — "build your muscle" — but already
  with a view to building on it without tumbling into chaos.

**Verdict.** The audience list on the event page fits well. I would add
consultants who want to move in this context — and I count myself among them,
even though I am exploring in "1 + AI" mode at the moment.

### "Agentic Engineering in Practice" — Henrik Kniberg / Abundly

| | |
|---|---|
| **Speaker** | Henrik Kniberg, Co-founder & Chief Scientist at Abundly AI |
| **Format** | Webinar, online, 80 minutes — <https://www.abundly.ai/events/2026-06-17/webinar-agentic-engineering-in-practice> |
| **Consumed** | 17 June 2026, live |
| **Themes** | Craft · Operating model |
| **Source material** | The event page carries the description and the recording. Linked and characterised here, not reproduced |

**What it argues.** How far agents can be taken in a real development workflow,
grounded in Abundly's own large and complex codebase rather than a prototype —
the claim being a tenfold productivity gain and a new platform version shipped
every day. It works through what holds up in practice: helping an agent navigate
a big codebase, keeping control of the architecture when agents write most of the
code, avoiding technical debt, and balancing agent autonomy with human oversight.
It then goes beyond coding — agents for backlog and release management — and
closes on what that means for team structure, processes and the engineering
craft.

**What I took from it.**

- The most important thing: it showed how easy the entry into agentic engineering
  is. Even more so with experience in software development.

**Where it landed.**

- Direct connection: the journey started the same day, 17 June 2026, with the
  repo split, the characterisation tests and the Maven wrapper (see
  `research/20260617-beginning.md`).
- Watching it was also a way to prepare myself a little for the "AI for Leaders"
  training that followed in July 2026.

**Verdict.** Absolutely worth recommending for developers and for leaders who
want to develop further. It can be a first step towards the Player Coach.

### "Generative AI in a Nutshell" — Henrik Kniberg

| | |
|---|---|
| **Author** | Henrik Kniberg — Certified Scrum Trainer with the Scrum Alliance, so a fellow CST; co-founder and Chief Scientist at Abundly, the platform Morten runs on |
| **Format** | Video, 18 minutes — <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2IK3DFHRFfw> |
| **Consumed** | February 2025 |
| **Themes** | Craft · Leadership model |
| **Source material** | Fully public: the video itself, the complete drawing as a PNG on `blog.crisp.se`, a book on Leanpub, and AI voice translations into eight languages, German among them. Linked and characterised here, not reproduced |

**What it argues.** A full day of AI course compressed into eighteen minutes of
drawing and talking, aimed at everyone rather than at engineers. It starts from
what a language model actually does — predict the next piece of text — and works
outward from there to prompting, autonomous agents, the limitations that follow
from the prediction mechanism, the origins of ChatGPT and AI-powered product
development. It closes on the role of humans and on mindset: the tool has to be
played with to be understood.

**What I took from it.**

- The video put my understanding of AI at the time on a solid basis and sparked a
  deeper interest. Prompting on ChatGPT was already standard for me by then.
- Kniberg is a trusted source for me. He is a fellow Certified Scrum Trainer, and
  I already liked his "Agile Product Ownership in a Nutshell" and "Spotify
  Engineering Culture" videos and reference them in trainings.

**Where it landed.**

- The video itself was not yet a driver to change anything. It created deeper
  understanding.

**Verdict.** Absolutely worth recommending for anyone who wants to understand
AI. Not outdated, even though some things have moved on since.
