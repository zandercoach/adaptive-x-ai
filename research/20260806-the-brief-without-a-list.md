## Technical Learnings

- A pattern repeated three times is not a convention. The series had delivered on
  its "Next up" line every time, so I proposed making it binding — and the rule
  was mine, not the practice's. The same evening produced a second instance:
  Christian decided once to skip ahead to the crew structure, and I wrote it into
  the repertoire as a rule about how structure gets published. Both times the
  observation was right and the generalization was not.
- Editing one post of a pair moves the other. Christian's rework of B6 changed
  what the shared beat is about, which changed the premise A22 was being written
  on. The pair is one artifact told twice, so the second draft cannot be finished
  before the first one is settled.
- An experiment needs its read-out designed in. The goal-oriented brief would
  otherwise only tell us whether we like the picture. Asking Morten to write into
  the pull request which idea he chose and why is what turns it into evidence
  about whether he understood the goal — and keeping A19's brief in the old
  element-by-element form gives the comparison something to stand against, in the
  same week and the same style.
- `REPERTOIRE.md` went stale inside a single session. The cadence note written
  at the start of the evening announced an empty queue, and by the end four
  drafts reached to 20.08. The same file, the same failure mode as on 01.08, and
  this time the drift took hours rather than a week.
- Length work is rhythm work, again. B6 came down from 525 to 400 words in
  several passes, and the passes that helped were the ones that removed a whole
  beat rather than the ones that tightened sentences.
- Pushing early has a schedule reason now. Morten finds image work by looking for
  a prompt with no PNG beside it on `main`, so a draft that is not pushed before
  Friday 08:00 simply is not seen by his run.

## Organizational Learnings

- Conventions come out of practice, out of discussing them together and putting
  them into force. They do not come out of observing a repetition.
