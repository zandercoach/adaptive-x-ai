# 20260906 - The Spec Answered for Me

## What I did

Sat the AI for Leaders Level 1 certificate exam with Henrik, the Agile Academy's
own AI agent, and passed with 30 out of 30. Thirty questions across the five
modules — the AI shift, how AI works, AI economics, governance, and the leader's
role — ten single-choice, twenty open, built live and tailored to the cohort. The
qualification, along with my standing as an Agile Academy Educator (Level 3) and
Agile Academy Ambassador (Level 2), allows the training to be delivered in-house
from here.

The conversation is recorded in `reference/ai-for-leaders/`, next to Henrik's own
notes on the cohort. His closing judgement was that the answers were grounded in
practice rather than recalled from the material: Morten Market's real
specification, client examples from a public agency and a small training company,
and personal use as a leader.

## Craft

- **The link was useless, the paste worked.** Asked to walk Morten through the
  model/instructions/tools/autonomy framework, I offered Henrik the GitHub URL to
  `MORTEN.md`. He has web search and no way to fetch a specific file. What went in
  instead was the generated instruction block — written for the agent, produced by
  the spec sync, and usable as exam evidence without a word changed.

- **The examiner noticed a recycled answer.** The cost-driver answer from the
  economics module came back out for the risk question, and Henrik named it
  instead of grading it: this one is about hallucinations, privacy, bias and
  over-reliance. The second attempt was the better answer.

- **Spec first, maybe the stronger model second.** When Claude Code or Morten
  deliver something poor, the first move is always the specification. The model
  is the engine, the context is direction and goal — a slower engine with precise
  context will most likely still arrive.

## Business Model

- The training is a door opener for further engagements — AI learning journeys,
  consulting. But leadership consulting, not pure delivery.

- What opens that door is the combination, not the exam on its own: the Level 1
  certificate sits on top of the Educator (Level 3) and Ambassador (Level 2)
  standing. A single qualification would not carry the in-house delivery.

- This training only works if the trainer brings their own practice and
  experience, ideally at all three levels: individual, team, organisation. In my
  case that may still need more than observations from the companies I work in.
  My agents will hopefully grow into a team with partners soon.

## Leadership Model

- "People problem" was the wrong category for my own assessment in July.
  "Leadership problem" was the right one from the start.

- The over-reliance risk shows up in my own work: I formulate less myself, and
  with all the reviewing of what Claude Code and Morten produce there is a real
  danger of becoming careless. On strategy and design I sometimes want human
  feedback — and I will go and get it.

## Operating Model

- The next step is widening from solo use to a real team with partners, where new
  agents may join. Two things get tested at once: whether Morten is of any use to
  the others, and whether the ladder itself carries — personal experiment, then
  team-level agent polished against governance and maintainability, then an
  org-level fleet.

- The three tripwires that end a personal subscription — real customer data,
  results going to clients, more than a handful of people — are not reached yet.
  The partners do not get access to all my data either, only to what the
  collaboration needs.

## Other Learnings

- I trusted the agent's verdict. Henrik was well prepared: he had accompanied me
  before and during the training, and had excellent context handed to him by the
  Agile Academy.

- I stand behind the certification — not because an agent interviewed and assessed
  me, but because the training beforehand was genuinely good.

- The reflex that using AI is cheating has not (yet) met me in my own profession.
