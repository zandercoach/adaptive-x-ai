# 20260731 - From a Marketing Team to One Crew

## What I did

- Took the insight from the "AI for Leaders" training — build *one* genuinely
  cross-functional team, one human plus n agents, rather than functional teams
  with an agent in them — and applied it to my own org chart the same week.
- Renamed the GitHub team `marketing` to `crew`, and gave Morten a role inside
  it: **Marketing & Sales**. The role is deliberately named wider than what he
  actually does; the sales half is an open slot, and a gap you can name is a gap
  you can close.
- Weighed `adaptive-x-ai` as an alternative name — the team named after the
  endeavor — and rejected it, precisely because more endeavors are coming (the
  zander.coach repositioning first). A team named after one forces either a second
  team per endeavor, with the only human as the shared resource in both, or a name
  that lies. The team is the constant, the endeavors are the backlog. Member and
  endeavor are separate entities in the Chronicler's own domain model; they stay
  separate in the organization that builds it.
- Recorded the commitment that travels with the name: **no second team.** The
  agents still on the backlog — the quality agent (IDEAS #9) and the
  dev-architect (#10) — join this crew and get a role; they do not get a team of
  their own.
- Split Slack along the same line. `#marketing` had been doing double duty since
  21.07 — escalation channel and everyday working channel at once, which is
  exactly the failure mode A24 warns about. It is now `#crew` for everything
  normal, plus a new `#crew-alerts` for escalation only, everyone in both. That
  closes the open question from 21.07 and IDEAS #18.
- Made escalation an **enumerated list** in Morten's instructions rather than a
  judgement call. Today it has exactly one entry: both slots of the coming week
  uncovered. "Repository unreachable" goes into the normal weekly report, not into
  the alerts channel. Every later agent inherits that rule.
- Dropped the 18.07 plan to switch escalation to a phone call. Its whole purpose
  was signal value, and a dedicated channel with notifications on every message
  buys the same thing without a new capability.
- Moved the weekly status to `#crew` **and** email — visible to the team, still
  in the inbox I actually read.
- Wrote it all down where it belongs: a new "One crew, two channels" section in
  `MORTEN.md`, the `team/` files, the README, IDEAS #18 (closed) and #19
  (superseded), and the training's "where it landed" note in `RESOURCES.md`.
  Then did the outside-repo half — the team rename in GitHub, the two Slack
  channels with Morten's app in both, and the grown instruction block mirrored
  into Abundly.
- Bringing the public page in line turned into a second piece of work. The team
  section on `docs/index.html` had quietly become a changelog — first it was
  called this, then that, and here is the alternative I rejected. Rewrote it to
  describe only what is, moved the path into the journal where it belongs, and
  said so on the page: nothing here was designed up front, this is the current
  result of an evolution through learning.
- Promoted that rule into `CLAUDE.md` as a section of its own, so it outlives the
  session and covers the resources page too — including the sibling failure mode,
  stale time markers like "since this week", one of which had been sitting in the
  team section since 23.07.
- Settled a loose end from 23.07 along the way: pushing to `main` as org owner
  succeeds, and GitHub reports afterwards that a rule was bypassed. Branch
  protection binds Morten and is advisory for me. My call is that the bypass stays
  as it is until further notice — recorded in `team/christian-zander.md` rather
  than left in a chat log.

## Technical Learnings

- Renaming the Slack channel beat creating a new one: `#marketing` → `#crew`
  keeps the working history and the app membership, so nothing had to be
  re-invited or re-linked. The new channel is the one that has no past worth
  keeping.
- The escalation mechanism is the notification setting, not tidiness. It only
  works while `#crew-alerts` stays almost empty — which is why what qualifies had
  to become an enumerated list instead of the agent's judgement. A criterion an
  agent evaluates will drift; a list will not.
- A channel replaced a capability. The 18.07 phone-call plan existed only to buy
  signal value; once the channel structure provides it, the capability was never
  needed. Worth checking before building: is the thing I want to add really a new
  capability, or a structure I don't have yet?
- Naming a role wider than its current scope turns a gap into a visible item.
  "Marketing & Sales" is a backlog entry disguised as a job title.
- The domain model of the thing we build turned out to answer a question about the
  organization that builds it: member and endeavor are separate entities in the
  Chronicler, and keeping them separate in the org chart is what made the
  team-name decision obvious.
- A public snapshot page drifts into a changelog by accretion, not by decision.
  Every single edit that says "this replaced that" is reasonable in the moment;
  the sum reads like a diff. The fix was to delete the path and write the rule
  down in `CLAUDE.md` so the next session doesn't re-accrete it.
- Branch protection is a control for whoever cannot bypass it and a note in the
  log for whoever can. Same rule, two different things, depending on the member.

## Organizational Learnings

- Writing "no second team" into the repo as a commitment — binding for agents
  that don't exist yet — was worth it because a second team is the relapse I
  would otherwise be free to make later.
- I want to keep the organization as small as possible and not establish team
  structures in advance that may turn out not to be necessary at all.
  `marketing` is the case in point: a structure named after a function before
  there was a need for it.
- And this one team should work with a clear focus, instead of doing many things
  at once.

## Leadership Perspective

- Replacing Morten's escalation judgement with an enumerated list is about a
  degree of maturity he does not have yet — and may never reach.
- Agents *can* potentially do a lot, and they would also do it. Clear boundaries
  are necessary — not necessarily because Morten *can't*, but because he
  explicitly *may not*.
