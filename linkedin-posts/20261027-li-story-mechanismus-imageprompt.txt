IMAGE STYLE GUIDE ("Zander Flipchart" — use for every future post)
====================================================================

Hand-drawn flipchart illustration, as sketched live by a trainer with markers: clean black marker outlines on a plain white background, simple friendly shapes, hand-lettered text. Human figures are always drawn with a body — a round head, an oval torso, and single-line arms and legs attached to that torso, each ending in a small round hand or foot, never a head with limbs hanging directly off it — and the posture carries the expression. This applies to the human figures only; the robot of this series keeps his own look (boxy head with antenna, segmented arms and legs) and is not changed by it. Accents only in classic flipchart marker colors — yellow, orange, green, blue, red — applied as wide, slightly uneven marker strokes. No gradients, no photorealism, no dark shading, plenty of white space. Light, warm, optimistic mood. Square composition. Captions are hand-lettered in black marker and underlined in black as well; the underline belongs to the lettering and is not a colour accent.


IMAGE PROMPT ("Mechanismus") — GOAL, NOT A CHECKLIST
==============================================================

This brief tells you what the picture is for. The staging is yours.

WHAT THE POST SAYS
Since July a rule in Christian's project said: whenever he changes his agent
Morten's instructions in the repository, he copies them by hand into Morten's
platform. It depended on somebody remembering, that somebody was Christian, and it
failed three times without anyone noticing, because a forgotten rule does not
announce itself. At the end of August Morten proposed the fix: the copy is
generated by a script on every change, cannot drift silently any more, and speaks
up in the team channel when something goes wrong. It was the third rule replaced
this way. Mechanism beats rule: a rule that lives on somebody remembering it fails
quietly, and quiet failure is the expensive kind; a mechanism that never says
anything soon becomes a rule again. Where the move stops is practical: Christian
does not yet know every mechanism that could replace a rule.

WHAT THE PICTURE SHOULD DO
Someone scrolling past should recognise the reminder trick everybody knows and
smile at it, and a second later see the friendlier alternative standing next to it:
a small mechanism that does the job by itself and makes itself heard. No mockery of
the human, no smug machine; the mood is "oh, of course".

WHAT IS FIXED
The style guide above. Square composition, white background. Two figures of this
series and no invented third: the stick-figure person and the robot. I chose both,
because the person carries the forgotten rule and the robot proposed the
mechanism. Both friendly. Any sheets or objects carry no readable words, numbers
or digits. No screens, no code, no speech bubbles, no question marks. Any
hand-lettering is German, since the post is German. A hand-lettered caption at the
bottom in black marker, underlined in black, reading exactly:
"Mechanismus schlägt Regel". As always, fetch two existing post PNGs from the same
folder as visual reference so the series keeps one look.

WHAT IS YOURS
The staging: what stands for the rule that has to be remembered (a string round a
finger is the obvious one, not required), what the mechanism looks like and how it
makes itself heard, where the two stand, and which marker colours do the work.
Keep it one clear moment with plenty of room around it.
