Adaptive × AI — Four hands on the same work

More from the lab notebook of my learning journey on adaptive-x-ai: for once I gave my marketing agent (does an agent have two hands?) an image brief that was not a checklist. It said what the post argues, what the picture should do to someone scrolling past, and the few things that are fixed. The motif was his to choose.

He came back with an answer I would have been glad to get from a person: both figures side by side, each wearing a name badge, and the two badges drawn identically, because identical badges make the picture read as belonging rather than as a promotion. He also named what he had dropped to get there. So goal orientation does transfer.

Everything around that went less well. He had delivered four images in one pull request. I asked for one change, closed the request an hour later without merging, and generated the pictures myself. That was no verdict on his work. I had lost the overview and wanted to start again, and I never said so anywhere.

Meanwhile he reworked all four on his branch, where nobody looks, because a closed pull request swallows the reaction. Two of us on the same four posts that evening, and the only channel between the strands was me.

So instead of tightening the process around all this, I handed the work over, and in stages. First he harvests post ideas from my journal, then he drafts, one pull request per post, and I review rather than rewrite. Morten drafts, I finish. The argument that turns a draft into a post stays with me. It only moves out of my session and into the review.

The last surprise was what the handover cost. The line I had taken for a safeguard, "image files only", was a sentence in his instructions and never a control. His credential could always write text. Branch protection and the code-owner review had been doing that job all along, so the handover needed no new access at all.

Full disclosure, as always: this is a learning report, not a how-to — what I set up, what nearly broke, and what I got wrong. Text and image were made together with AI, and I reviewed both before they went out. The work can be shared. Being accountable for it cannot.
