# VOICE.md — how Christian sounds

The core. It holds for **every medium that speaks as Christian**: posts, the
website, slide decks, handouts, proposals, mail that goes out. Like the
`CLAUDE.md` beside it, this file is the bracket over everything; the more
specific versions sit with their channel and **supplement** it — they do not
contradict it.

Built from five posts Christian wrote himself (appendix), a style interview on
2026-07-12, and the decisions that have since come out of applying it to other
media.

## The cascade

| Channel | Supplement | What it governs |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | `projects/adaptive-x-ai/linkedin-posts/VOICE.md` | Two tracks, hashtags, first comment, length, AI disclosure, file naming, workflow. It also carries a dated **transcript of this file**, because Morten reads it over GitHub and cannot see the workspace root |
| Website | `brand/positioning/VOICE.md` | Gender form, dose of self-irony, capitalisation of the address, what does not carry over from LinkedIn |
| Slide decks | `material/decks/VOICE.md` | Who speaks on the slide, gender form, capitalisation of the address; the slide carries fragments and the voice lives in the director's notes, so the dose is per slide instead of per text |

**What a supplement does not say is governed from here.** A supplement may make
a rule concrete and set its dose. Only this file can change the core — and then
for every channel at once.

## When an exception is justified

An exception must **name a property of the medium that a post does not have.**
No comment field, so no resolution "in the next few days". No algorithm that
downranks links, so links in the body. Forty words per slide instead of two
hundred and fifty per post. The reader arrives on purpose instead of mid-scroll.

Not justified: "it fits better here", "it reads more professional".

**The counter-test:** if the reason would hold for a post just as well, it is
not an exception but a change to the core. Then it belongs here, not in the
supplement. This rule is why the voice does not soften channel by channel
without anyone ever having decided to change it.

Every exception lives in its supplement, with a reason and a date. An exception
that exists only in the delivered document — in the header of a draft, in a
review comment — is a side channel: the next medium never sees it and invents
it again.

## What the guide governs

**Only what the draft contributed.** Quoted material, text from a source and
Christian's own wording stay verbatim, even where they break a rule: the
em-dash inside a quote belongs to the source, not to the drafter. This was the
governing rule of the slide-deck check on 2026-08-26, and it holds everywhere.

**A surface where someone else speaks is not a surface that speaks as
Christian.** On seven of the eight SfAO slides the association speaks, not him;
scene, humility grammar and self-irony are absent there and are meant to be. The
stance and the voice markers apply where he speaks in his own name. The AI tells
apply everywhere, because they are about the drafting, not about the persona.

## Stance

- **Address: du, not Sie.** And the distinction that is not mechanical: **du**
  where the reader is meant as a person; **ihr/euer** where the organisation is
  meant ("euer Geschäftsmodell", "was ihr mit KI macht"). Whether it is
  capitalised is the channel's call.
- **Gender-inclusive German, always.** Which form — colon, both nouns spelled
  out, feminine forms — is the channel's call.
- **Self-irony is required, not permitted.** Every surface that speaks as
  Christian carries at least one gesture of humility or a wink. *Exactly one* —
  two are a shtick. Without it the text reads like a good template and not like
  him; that is the difference the website check made visible on 2026-08-25. The
  irony is gentle and at his own expense, never snark, never at others'.
- **Humility is built into the grammar:** "durfte ich erleben", "habe ich mir
  endlich die Zeit genommen", "learning by doing". He frames himself as a
  learner even where he is demonstrating expertise. Never guru posture.
- **Credit is specific, not praise.** Not "great training!" but what exactly the
  person did well. Co-facilitators, participants, friends get their names.
- **He makes himself the subject of the lesson** instead of selling: "my
  coaching clients deserve a rested, motivated Christian".
- **Accountability cannot be shared.** Where AI wrote along, that is said, along
  with the fact that he reviewed it. The form is so far decided for posts only
  (2026-08-24, see the LinkedIn supplement); for the website and slide decks it
  is open and noted below.

## Voice markers

- **Scene before message.** The story first, the point arrives late and lightly.
  No "3 lessons from…".
- **Methodical depth without dumbing down.** He walks through actual method
  steps in one precise paragraph and trusts readers to follow.
- **He opens with the sceptic's objection and refutes it with lived
  experience:** "Vorschulkinder können doch noch gar nicht schreiben … wie
  sollen sie da an unserer Wochenplanung mitwirken?" A rhetorical question works
  when it voices a real doubt the text then answers — not as bait.
- **Family life is legitimate material.** The kids appear (by age, not name) as
  living examples of team practices; the bridge into the professional is spelled
  out.
- **Real play is allowed** — a riddle with a promised resolution, an announced
  series. What is banned is the cheap bait, not the play.
- **Titles may carry a bilingual pun** ("LEGO® — are you serious?"), not a
  clickbait hook.
- **✅️ as the bullet** is his native form in German recaps.
- **Emoji sparingly.** His natural set: 😉 ;-) ✅️ 😄 — usually a self-ironic
  wink near the end. How many is the channel's call.
- **He addresses the crowd as "ihr"** ("Anbei seht ihr…"); exclamation marks
  carry warmth, ";-)!" happens.
- **English** is fluent, warm, slightly formal in idiom ("Last but not least"),
  the sentences take their time.

## What gives a text away as AI-written

- **Em-dashes (—).** The single biggest tell. Written as a density so the rule
  travels: **at most one per paragraph, on average about one per 120 words.**
  The rest is rewritten with commas, colons or full stops, whichever the
  sentence carries. Where the dash marks the strongest point of the paragraph,
  it stays.

  *Derivation, so the number does not look invented:* the old post rule ("once
  or twice per post") applied at 120–350 words per post. The website state
  signed off on 2026-08-25 sits at 18 dashes across 2,229 words of valid text,
  one per about 124. Both times the density is the invariant; the count was an
  accident of the format.
- **"Not X, but Y".** The construction needs a **real distinction**, otherwise
  it is rhetoric. And it must not cluster: three paragraphs opening identically
  with "Das ist keine…" are what make it audible. Rebuild rather than delete —
  "Das ist keine Frage von Mut, sondern von Statik" becomes "Das ist Statik,
  nicht Mut".
- **Banned:** "game-changer", "Let that sink in", emoji-bullet listicles,
  engagement bait ("Agree?", "Thoughts?"), perfectly parallel three-part
  sentences everywhere. German equivalents equally banned ("Lass das mal
  sacken").
- **Endings** vary, with no formula; quiet and never clickbait. A soft takeaway,
  a generous thank-you, a wink at his own expense. Never a rhetorical question
  fishing for comments.

## Open

- **AI disclosure outside LinkedIn.** For posts, wording and placement have been
  settled since 2026-08-24. Whether the website and slide decks carry one, and
  in what form, is open. Do not derive it from the post rule: a convention comes
  out of an agreement, not out of noticing that it is already done that way
  elsewhere.

## What applies on LinkedIn only

- **Hashtags:** woven into the sentence (e.g. "meinen eigenen #Rhythmus"), plus
  any *new* terms parked at the bottom. Few, not a wall.
- **No external links in the post body** (LinkedIn downranks them). Refer to
  "link in the first comment" / "Link im ersten Kommentar" instead, and every
  draft file carries a paste-ready `FIRST COMMENT` / `ERSTER KOMMENTAR` block
  with the URL, to be posted right after publishing. The journey anchor in the
  body is "my learning journey on adaptive-x-ai" (German: "meine Lernreise
  adaptive-x-ai") — the domain name without the TLD, so readers see the brand
  but LinkedIn sees no link.
- **Length:** whatever the story needs, shorter is better. Leadership stories
  tend ~120–250 words; journey reports may run longer (250–350) per the format.
  **These are limits, not descriptions (2026-08-30).** The reports of 10./24./31.08
  ran 398, 399 and 398 words and Christian read all three as too long, his own
  drafting included. The range stays; the practice moves back to it. A report over
  350 words is trimmed before it is scheduled, and the pass that helps drops a
  whole beat rather than tightening sentences (20260806).
- **AI disclosure (agreed 2026-08-24, not from the interview):** every post states
  that it was made together with AI and that Christian reviewed it before it went
  out. The claim being made is accountability: the work is shared, being answerable
  for it is not, and the review is what makes that sentence true. Journey reports
  carry it inside the closing "Full disclosure" line; leadership stories carry it
  as their last paragraph, after the journey anchor and before the hashtags, so
  that a closing wink is not blunted by it. There it may be introduced as an aside
  ("Übrigens:") — it is a footnote to the story, not part of it. The English
  wording may use *accountable*, which is the precise word there; German collapses
  the distinction into *Verantwortung* and simply keeps it. First used on 24./27.08:
  "Text and image were made together with AI, and I reviewed both before they went
  out. The work can be shared. Being accountable for it cannot." /
  "Text und Bild sind gemeinsam mit KI entstanden, ich habe beides geprüft, bevor
  es rausging. Die Arbeit lässt sich teilen, die Verantwortung dafür nicht."
  Vary the wording, keep the two claims: made with AI, and reviewed by him.

## Workflow

Christian voice-checks and posts himself; the final "post" click is always his,
and so is the argument that turns a draft into a post. Who writes the first draft
depends on the track: from 2026-08-28 Morten drafts the Track B reports, and from
2026-08-30 the Track A stories as well, one pull request per post. The gate on the
second half was that the first draft hold up, and it did — one rework, merged,
scheduled. Track A is where the difference between the tracks stops being a
language setting: the German story is shorter, addressed differently, and puts the
AI disclosure in another place, all of which this file already said and none of
which an agent had ever written.

File naming: `YYYYMMDD-li-report-<topic>.txt` for journey reports,
`YYYYMMDD-li-story-<topic>.txt` for leadership stories (+ `-imageprompt.txt`), where
**`YYYYMMDD` is the intended posting date** (the Mon/Tue or Thu/Fri slot), not the
drafting date, and `<topic>` carries no hyphens; images in the Zander Flipchart
style, whose style guide lives in `IMAGE-STYLE.md` — that file is the source a new
imageprompt copies its header from, and the header of an older one is a record of
what was true when that post was written.
