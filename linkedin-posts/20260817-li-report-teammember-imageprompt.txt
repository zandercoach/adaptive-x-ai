IMAGE STYLE GUIDE ("Zander Flipchart" — use for every future post)
====================================================================

Hand-drawn flipchart illustration, as sketched live by a trainer with markers: clean black marker outlines on a plain white background, simple friendly shapes, stick figures with round heads and expressive posture, hand-lettered text. Accents only in classic flipchart marker colors — yellow, orange, green, blue, red — applied as wide, slightly uneven marker strokes. No gradients, no photorealism, no dark shading, plenty of white space. Light, warm, optimistic mood. Square composition.


IMAGE PROMPT ("Morten gets an identity of his own") — GOAL, NOT A CHECKLIST
=======================================================================

This brief is written differently on purpose. It does not describe the scene element by element. It tells you what the picture is for, and leaves the motif to you.

WHAT THE POST SAYS
For a week the agent did real work, but all of it was signed with someone else's name: his credential belonged to his human colleague, so in the history the two were indistinguishable. Then the setup was rebuilt as a team, and he joined it with his own account, his own credential and his own identity. He is now a recognized member, not a pair of borrowed hands.

WHAT THE PICTURE SHOULD DO
Someone scrolling past should understand before reading a word that a small robot has become a member of a team, and should find that warm rather than technical. The feeling to aim at is quiet belonging, with a bit of pride in it. Avoid anything that reads as hierarchy, promotion or a ceremony.

WHAT IS FIXED
The style guide above. Square composition, white background. The small friendly stick-figure robot that carries this series is the agent, and the stick-figure person is his human colleague. A hand-lettered caption at the bottom in black marker, underlined, reading exactly: "An identity of his own". As always, fetch two existing post PNGs from the same folder as visual reference so the series keeps one look.

WHAT IS YOURS
The motif, the composition, and which single detail carries the message. Pick one clear idea and let it breathe, rather than assembling several.

TWO REQUESTS
If the goal above is unclear or you can read it in more than one way, say so in the pull request description instead of guessing.
And write one or two sentences into the pull request description explaining which idea you chose and why. That is the point of this experiment: this brief gives you a goal instead of a list, and we want to see what you do with the freedom.
