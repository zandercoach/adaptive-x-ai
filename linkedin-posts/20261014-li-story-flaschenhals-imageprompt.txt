IMAGE STYLE GUIDE ("Zander Flipchart" — use for every future post)
====================================================================

Hand-drawn flipchart illustration, as sketched live by a trainer with markers: clean black marker outlines on a plain white background, simple friendly shapes, hand-lettered text. Human figures are always drawn with a body — a round head, an oval torso, and single-line arms and legs attached to that torso, each ending in a small round hand or foot, never a head with limbs hanging directly off it — and the posture carries the expression. This applies to the human figures only; the robot of this series keeps his own look (boxy head with antenna, segmented arms and legs) and is not changed by it. Accents only in classic flipchart marker colors — yellow, orange, green, blue, red — applied as wide, slightly uneven marker strokes. No gradients, no photorealism, no dark shading, plenty of white space. Light, warm, optimistic mood. Square composition. Captions are hand-lettered in black marker and underlined in black as well; the underline belongs to the lettering and is not a colour accent.


IMAGE PROMPT ("Flaschenhals") — GOAL, NOT A CHECKLIST
==============================================================

This brief tells you what the picture is for. The staging is yours.

WHAT THE POST SAYS
For a whole day in early September Christian was mostly the interface between his
agents and his repository: a report, a question back, corrections, a retraction of
his own, a decision and new rules, seventeen commits, every one of them through
him. The more his agents deliver, the clearer it gets: the bottleneck is him. The
next day showed another way: the agent picked a topic himself, named that its
scene had already been told and offered alternatives, which turned his draft into
a recommendation rather than a finished task. What follows is working towards
goals, with agents deciding more for themselves inside the right conditions, while
the responsibility for steps and result stays with Christian.

WHAT THE PICTURE SHOULD DO
Someone scrolling past should smile at the person standing in his own way, and
recognise the jam from their own desk a second later. Self-ironic, not stressed:
the person is not suffering and the robot is not to blame. The picture carries the
bottleneck only; the way out is left to the text.

WHAT IS FIXED
The style guide above. Square composition, white background. Two figures of this
series and no invented third: the stick-figure person and the robot, both
friendly. No readable words, numbers or digits anywhere except the caption. Any
hand-lettering is German, since the post is German. A hand-lettered caption at the
bottom in black marker, underlined in black, reading exactly:
"Der Flaschenhals bin ich". As always, fetch two existing post PNGs from the same
folder as visual reference so the series keeps one look.

WHAT IS YOURS
The staging: how the bottleneck is drawn, where the two stand, what the work
looks like and which marker colours do it. Keep it one clear moment with plenty of
room around it.
