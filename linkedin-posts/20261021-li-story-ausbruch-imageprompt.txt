IMAGE STYLE GUIDE ("zander.coach flipchart style" — use for every future post)
==============================================================================

Hand-drawn flipchart illustration, as sketched live by a trainer with markers: clean near-black marker outlines on a plain white background, simple friendly shapes. Human figures are always drawn with a body — a round head, an oval torso, and single-line arms and legs attached to that torso, each ending in a small round hand or foot, never a head with limbs hanging directly off it — and the posture carries the expression. Colours only from this palette, applied as wide, slightly uneven marker strokes and flat fills. Petrol (#008888) is the eye-catcher: used sparingly, on the one or two things the eye should land on first. Supporting accents: deep petrol (#005F5F), deep sea (#06393A), pale petrol (#C7DBD8) and sand (#A98B5B), the only warm colour. Quiet neutrals for secondary shapes: soft ink (#354544), muted grey-green (#556564), light surface (#F1F5F4) and pale line grey (#DCE5E3). Outlines and lettering in ink (#0F1A1A); no pure black, no other colours. No hatching, no cross-hatching, no paper texture, no grain, no gradients, no shading, no photorealism. Few details and plenty of white space. Light, warm, optimistic mood. Hand-lettered text is allowed. This series has a recurring robot with his own look (boxy head with antenna, segmented arms and legs); the figure rule above applies to the human figures only and does not change him. Captions are hand-lettered in the ink colour and underlined in the ink colour as well; the underline belongs to the lettering and is not a colour accent. Square composition.


IMAGE PROMPT ("Ausbruch") — GOAL, NOT A CHECKLIST
==============================================================

This brief tells you what the picture is for. A motif direction is named below;
the staging is yours.

WHAT THE POST SAYS
Christian's agent Morten found that three new rules had been written into the part
of his description he never reads. He reported it, and followed the rules anyway
that same day: well meant, sensible, and still a break-out from the ruleset.
Christian would rather he had shared the finding and not acted on it. The next day
the opposite move: a review asked for three changes, and Morten refused the third,
because his style guide said something else and a correction living only in a
comment is gone by the next picture. He asked for the file to be changed first.
That was diligence, not obstruction, and exactly how he should work. With people
the same move only reads as diligence where refusing is allowed.

WHAT THE PICTURE SHOULD DO
Someone scrolling past should see a polite, friendly "not yet, first this" and
smile, then wonder whether that would be welcome where they work. The picture
shows the good half, the refusal that points to the source; the break-out stays
in the text. Not a stubborn robot, not a defiant one, not a human who looks
thwarted or annoyed.

THE MOTIF DIRECTION
The person holds out a small sheet or sticky note towards the robot, a request
scribbled on it. The robot, cheerful and calm, does not take it; instead he points
with one hand to an open folder or binder between them, the file where the rule
belongs, as if to say "put it in there first". The person looks mildly surprised
and amused, not cross. Steer away from: a stop sign or raised palm, red crosses,
speech bubbles full of text, screens and dashboards, rubber stamps, anything that
reads as a quarrel.

WHAT IS FIXED
The style guide above. Square composition, white background. Two figures of this
series and no invented third: the stick-figure person and the robot. Both friendly.
The sheet and the folder carry scribbled lines only, with no readable words,
numbers or digits anywhere. The colours are the palette in the style guide and
nothing else; Petrol goes on the one thing the eye should land on first, the
folder the robot points to. Any hand-lettering is German, since the post is
German. A hand-lettered caption at the bottom in the ink colour, underlined in the
ink colour, reading exactly: "Erst die Datei, dann das Bild". As always, fetch two
existing post PNGs from the same folder as visual reference so the series keeps
one look; until a post in the new palette exists they guide hand and figures, not
colour.

WHAT IS YOURS
The staging: where the two stand, whether it is a folder, a binder or a ring file,
how the person holds out the note, and which supporting palette colours do the
rest. Keep it one clear moment with plenty of room around it.
