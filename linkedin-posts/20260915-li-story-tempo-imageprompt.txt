IMAGE STYLE GUIDE ("Zander Flipchart" — use for every future post)
====================================================================

Hand-drawn flipchart illustration, as sketched live by a trainer with markers: clean black marker outlines on a plain white background, simple friendly shapes, hand-lettered text. Human figures are always drawn with a body — a round head, an oval torso, and single-line arms and legs attached to that torso, each ending in a small round hand or foot, never a head with limbs hanging directly off it — and the posture carries the expression. This applies to the human figures only; the robot of this series keeps his own look (boxy head with antenna, segmented arms and legs) and is not changed by it. Accents only in classic flipchart marker colors — yellow, orange, green, blue, red — applied as wide, slightly uneven marker strokes. No gradients, no photorealism, no dark shading, plenty of white space. Light, warm, optimistic mood. Square composition. Captions are hand-lettered in black marker and underlined in black as well; the underline belongs to the lettering and is not a colour accent.


IMAGE PROMPT ("Tempo ist eine Eigenschaft des Weges") — GOAL, NOT A CHECKLIST
==============================================================

This brief tells you what the picture is for and leaves the motif to you. It does not describe the scene element by element.

WHAT THE POST SAYS
An insight from a training on a Wednesday stood in Christian's own org chart by the Friday: one genuinely cross-functional team instead of functional ones, the team renamed, Morten given a role inside it, Slack split into a working channel and an escalation channel, the instruction block rewritten. Three days. Not because he worked fast, but because the change had to pass through nobody: there was no one to convince, and undoing the structure costs him almost nothing. In a larger organisation the identical step touches the status quo, the distribution of power and the standing of the people inside it, so resistance is to be expected, open and subversive, and he now budgets for it instead of reading it as a motivation problem. Speed is therefore not a property of the people involved. It is a property of what a change has to pass through: how many people have to be convinced, and what it costs to reverse the rebuild. The close is at his own expense — his three days are a property of his company size rather than a leadership achievement.

WHAT THE PICTURE SHOULD DO
Someone scrolling past should see, before reading a word, that the same change can have a short path or a long one, and that this is what decides the pace. The mood is matter-of-fact and warm. Steer away from: bureaucracy-as-complaint imagery (stamps, towers of files, red tape), angry or exasperated faces, clocks, stopwatches, speedometers and racing imagery, and any composition that makes the slow side look stupid rather than structured.

WHAT IS FIXED
The style guide above. Square composition, white background. Both figures of this series: the stick-figure person and the small friendly stick-figure robot, exactly these two and no invented third. They belong on the short-path side, because the crew of this story is the two of them — the long path is a structure and not a group of people, so it carries no figures at all. Any hand-lettering in the picture is German, since the post is German. A hand-lettered caption at the bottom in black marker, underlined in black, reading exactly: "Tempo ist eine Eigenschaft des Weges". Apart from the caption the picture carries no words. As always, fetch two existing post PNGs from the same folder as visual reference so the series keeps one look.

WHAT IS YOURS
The motif, the composition, and which single detail carries the message. Pick one clear idea and let it breathe rather than assembling several. How the two paths are shown, and what the long one passes through, is yours to decide.
