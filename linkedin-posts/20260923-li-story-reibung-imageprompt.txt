IMAGE STYLE GUIDE ("Zander Flipchart" — use for every future post)
====================================================================

Hand-drawn flipchart illustration, as sketched live by a trainer with markers: clean black marker outlines on a plain white background, simple friendly shapes, hand-lettered text. Human figures are always drawn with a body — a round head, an oval torso, and single-line arms and legs attached to that torso, each ending in a small round hand or foot, never a head with limbs hanging directly off it — and the posture carries the expression. This applies to the human figures only; the robot of this series keeps his own look (boxy head with antenna, segmented arms and legs) and is not changed by it. Accents only in classic flipchart marker colors — yellow, orange, green, blue, red — applied as wide, slightly uneven marker strokes. No gradients, no photorealism, no dark shading, plenty of white space. Light, warm, optimistic mood. Square composition. Captions are hand-lettered in black marker and underlined in black as well; the underline belongs to the lettering and is not a colour accent.


IMAGE PROMPT ("Reibung") — GOAL, NOT A CHECKLIST
==============================================================

This brief tells you what the picture is for and leaves the motif to you. It does not describe the scene element by element.

WHAT THE POST SAYS
Christian changed the design direction of his brand twice this summer, and both times it was done quickly, because a revision with an agent costs nothing. Had a person worked on it, somebody would have asked why, pushed back, and put a third proposal on the table that neither of them had brought into the room. That is what falls away: a revision that costs nothing costs nobody anything, and what costs nothing brings nothing either. He now notices it in himself. He formulates less than he did in June and reviews more of what his two agents produce, and the better that gets, the more lightly he reviews. The risk is not that the output gets worse, it is that his own attention gets cheaper without him noticing, and "then I will review harder" is an answer he does not believe from himself. What he does instead is fetch feedback on strategy and design from people, deliberately, because friction is part of what a colleague contributes and the uncomfortable conversation is where the third idea comes from. In teams the same holds without any AI: whoever makes disagreement expensive gets fast agreement and few ideas.

WHAT THE PICTURE SHOULD DO
Someone scrolling past should feel the effortlessness first and sense a second later that something has gone missing with it. Ease, then loss. Not a warning, not a telling-off, and not a picture that makes the agent the culprit. Steer away from: broken or grinding cogs and gears, sparks, flames, sandpaper, two figures arguing, angry or sad faces, red warning signs, screens, dashboards, and speech bubbles full of text.

WHAT IS FIXED
The style guide above. Square composition, white background. Two figures of this series and no invented third: the stick-figure person and the robot. The colleague who is missing must not be drawn, not even faintly or in outline — the picture shows what is there, and the absence has to be legible from that. Both figures are friendly and content; the loss is in the situation, not on a face. No numbers, digits or percentages anywhere. Any hand-lettering is German, since the post is German. A hand-lettered caption at the bottom in black marker, underlined in black, reading exactly: "Was nichts kostet, bringt nichts". Apart from the caption the picture carries no words. As always, fetch two existing post PNGs from the same folder as visual reference so the series keeps one look.

WHAT IS YOURS
The motif and the composition: how you show a pull that meets nothing — a slack rope, a lean against something that is not there, a handover with nothing coming back; the posture of the person, which carries the whole thing; and which marker colour does the work. Pick one clear idea and let it breathe rather than assembling several.
