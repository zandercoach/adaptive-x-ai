IMAGE STYLE GUIDE ("Zander Flipchart" — use for every future post)
====================================================================

Hand-drawn flipchart illustration, as sketched live by a trainer with markers: clean black marker outlines on a plain white background, simple friendly shapes, hand-lettered text. Human figures are always drawn with a body — a round head, an oval torso, and thin arms and legs attached to that torso, never a head with limbs hanging directly off it — and the posture carries the expression. This applies to the human figures only; the robot of this series keeps his own look (boxy head with antenna, segmented arms and legs) and is not changed by it. Accents only in classic flipchart marker colors — yellow, orange, green, blue, red — applied as wide, slightly uneven marker strokes. No gradients, no photorealism, no dark shading, plenty of white space. Light, warm, optimistic mood. Square composition. Captions are hand-lettered in black marker and underlined in black as well; the underline belongs to the lettering and is not a colour accent.


IMAGE PROMPT ("Gib das Ziel, nicht die Liste") — GOAL, NOT A CHECKLIST
==============================================================

This brief tells you what the picture is for and leaves the motif to you. It does not describe the scene element by element.

WHAT THE POST SAYS
An image brief went to the agent as a goal instead of a checklist: what the post says, what the picture should do to someone scrolling past, and the few things that are fixed. The motif was left to him, with one request attached — ask instead of guessing if the goal reads two ways. What came back was a motif the human would not have thought of, with a reason for it and a list of what had been left out to get there. The reflex to write the list comes from worrying about the result, but a list never shows you how far someone gets on their own. Giving the goal is only the second step: enabling comes first, and enabling is largely documentation, since every new team member starts like a new session. A brief without context is not trust, it is abandonment. What makes the attempt cheap is the review — a proposal you can turn down costs nothing — and the leader's own role moves from knowing better to judging: is it right, does it land, what do I risk. The close is honest rather than triumphant: with people he still catches himself holding the list.

WHAT THE PICTURE SHOULD DO
Someone scrolling past should feel the difference between handing over a goal and handing over a list, and should read the goal as the more generous of the two, not as the vaguer one. The mood is warm and collegial, no supervision, no teaching. Steer away from: bar charts and dashboards, arrows hitting a target as a success metaphor for performance, ticked-off checklists as the whole idea, and any composition where one figure stands above or in front of the other.

WHAT IS FIXED
The style guide above. Square composition, white background. The two figures of this series carry the picture: the stick-figure person and the small friendly stick-figure robot, exactly these two and no invented third. They face each other as equals. Any hand-lettering in the picture is German, since the post is German. A hand-lettered caption at the bottom in black marker, underlined in black, reading exactly: "Gib das Ziel, nicht die Liste". As always, fetch two existing post PNGs from the same folder as visual reference so the series keeps one look.

WHAT IS YOURS
The motif, the composition, and which single detail carries the message. Pick one clear idea and let it breathe, rather than assembling several.
