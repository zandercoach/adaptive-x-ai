# Zander Flipchart — the image style guide

**This file is the source. The copies are records.**

Every `*-imageprompt.txt` in this folder carries this style guide in its header,
because a brief has to be one self-contained file: whoever generates an image
fetches one raw file and works from it. Those copies are snapshots. They are
never brought up to date, because an older brief describes the image that was
generated from it — a rule taken on 2026-08-10 and the reason the archive is
allowed to disagree with this file.

What changed on 2026-08-24 is only where a *new* copy comes from. Until then the
current style guide was whichever imageprompt file had the newest date, so
finding the truth meant sorting filenames. Now it is here.

## How to change the style

1. Change it here first, with a dated line in the history below saying what and why.
2. Then copy the block into the imageprompt of the **current** post, the next one
   to be written or generated. Never backwards through the archive.
3. A correction that lives only in a Slack message or a review comment is not a
   style change. It reaches the next image only from this file.

## The block, to be copied verbatim

Everything between the markers goes into the header of a new `*-imageprompt.txt`,
under the heading `IMAGE STYLE GUIDE ("Zander Flipchart" — use for every future post)`.

<!-- style-block:start -->
Hand-drawn flipchart illustration, as sketched live by a trainer with markers: clean black marker outlines on a plain white background, simple friendly shapes, hand-lettered text. Human figures are always drawn with a body — a round head, an oval torso, and thin arms and legs attached to that torso, never a head with limbs hanging directly off it — and the posture carries the expression. This applies to the human figures only; the robot of this series keeps his own look (boxy head with antenna, segmented arms and legs) and is not changed by it. Accents only in classic flipchart marker colors — yellow, orange, green, blue, red — applied as wide, slightly uneven marker strokes. No gradients, no photorealism, no dark shading, plenty of white space. Light, warm, optimistic mood. Square composition. Captions are hand-lettered in black marker and underlined in black as well; the underline belongs to the lettering and is not a colour accent.
<!-- style-block:end -->

## History

- **2026-06-17.** The first two posts had a different look entirely: black and
  white pen-and-ink in a fantasy chronicle style, high contrast, dense
  crosshatching. It belonged to the Chronicler's subject matter rather than to
  Christian's, and it did not survive the series finding its own voice.
- **2026-07-09.** "Zander Flipchart" established, and stable for five weeks: hand
  drawn as if sketched live by a trainer with markers, black outlines on white,
  accents only in flipchart marker colours, plenty of white space, square.
- **2026-08-10.** Human figures always have a body — round head, oval torso, limbs
  attached to that torso, never a head with limbs hanging off it. Written after a
  review on 08.08 asked for it and the first version of the correction, "oval
  torsos" in the shared header, silently reached the robot as well. Hence the
  second half of the rule: it applies to the human figures only, and the robot
  keeps his own look. In force from the post of 20.08 onward, not backwards.
- **2026-08-24.** Captions are hand-lettered in black marker and underlined in
  black as well; the underline belongs to the lettering rather than being a
  colour accent. Morten asked the question in pull request #5 — the brief said
  "caption at the bottom in black marker, underlined" and left the underline
  open, and the series carried both. He decided for black and said why, which
  was right on both counts.
