IMAGE STYLE GUIDE ("Zander Flipchart" — use for every future post)
====================================================================

Hand-drawn flipchart illustration, as sketched live by a trainer with markers: clean black marker outlines on a plain white background, simple friendly shapes, hand-lettered text. Human figures are always drawn with a body — a round head, an oval torso, and single-line arms and legs attached to that torso, each ending in a small round hand or foot, never a head with limbs hanging directly off it — and the posture carries the expression. This applies to the human figures only; the robot of this series keeps his own look (boxy head with antenna, segmented arms and legs) and is not changed by it. Accents only in classic flipchart marker colors — yellow, orange, green, blue, red — applied as wide, slightly uneven marker strokes. No gradients, no photorealism, no dark shading, plenty of white space. Light, warm, optimistic mood. Square composition. Captions are hand-lettered in black marker and underlined in black as well; the underline belongs to the lettering and is not a colour accent.


IMAGE PROMPT ("Feedback") — GOAL, NOT A CHECKLIST
==============================================================

This brief tells you what the picture is for. The motif direction is named below,
because the last open brief could not be read (review of 18.09); the staging is
yours.

WHAT THE POST SAYS
At the end of August Christian's agent Morten drafted a post for him for the first
time. Christian took it over and finished it himself, and none of that finish
reached the agent: once the draft was taken over, his part was done. They did not
fix it by making feedback something Christian owes him. The agent fetches it
himself: before he writes the next draft, he reads what became of his last one and
notes what he takes from it. He can do that at any moment, so he simply does,
without asking whether it suits and without minding what was cut. With people this
pull does not carry on its own. Before someone fetches feedback for themselves it
takes a few things, above all the safety that the relationship is constructive and
supportive and that the feedback will move their own development forward. Asking
for feedback costs something, and how much is decided by the culture of the team.
Where that safety is missing, the pull fails quietly.

WHAT THE PICTURE SHOULD DO
Someone scrolling past should see how easy it is for the agent first, and a second
later ask themselves whether the people around them would do the same. The ease is
what is drawn; the question is left to the caption and the reader. Not a telling-off,
not a picture that makes the human look like a bad boss or the agent look smug.

THE MOTIF DIRECTION
The robot helps himself: he picks his own returned draft, a sheet with marker
corrections on it, out of a tray or off a pile on the person's side, cheerfully and
without being handed it. The person is relaxed and friendly, busy with something
else or just watching, and is neither handing it over nor being asked. Steer away
from: speech bubbles full of text, question marks raining down, screens and
dashboards, report cards and grades, thumbs-up icons, a rubber stamp (the last post
used one), and anything that looks like an interrogation.

WHAT IS FIXED
The style guide above. Square composition, white background. Two figures of this
series and no invented third: the stick-figure person and the robot. No other
person is drawn, not even faintly; the human side of the question lives in the
caption. Both figures friendly. The corrections on the sheet are marker strokes,
crossings-out and a few lines only, with no readable words, numbers or digits
anywhere. Any hand-lettering is German, since the post is German. A hand-lettered
caption at the bottom in black marker, underlined in black, reading exactly:
"Er holt es sich einfach". As always, fetch two existing post PNGs from the same
folder as visual reference so the series keeps one look.

WHAT IS YOURS
The staging: where the two stand, what the person is busy with, whether it is a
tray, a pile or a folder, and which marker colour does the work (red for the
corrections on the sheet is the obvious one, but not required). Keep it one clear
moment with plenty of room around it.
