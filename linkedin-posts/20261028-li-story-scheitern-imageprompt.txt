IMAGE STYLE GUIDE ("zander.coach flipchart style" — use for every future post)
================================================================================

Hand-drawn flipchart illustration, as sketched live by a trainer with markers: clean near-black marker outlines on a plain white background, simple friendly shapes. Human figures are always drawn with a body — a round head, an oval torso, and single-line arms and legs attached to that torso, each ending in a small round hand or foot, never a head with limbs hanging directly off it — and the posture carries the expression. Each figure is about five heads tall: a large round head, a tall narrow oval torso about one and a half heads long, long straight legs. Faces are two dot eyes and a small curved smile, no nose, the head often turned slightly to one side; hair is a few loose marker strokes that tell the figures apart. Torsos are filled flat or left white. One even line weight throughout, like a medium marker at about half a percent of the image width, never thicker for emphasis. Colours only from this palette, applied as wide, slightly uneven marker strokes and flat fills. Petrol (#008888) is the eye-catcher: used sparingly, on the one or two things the eye should land on first. Supporting accents: deep petrol (#005F5F), deep sea (#06393A), pale petrol (#C7DBD8) and sand (#A98B5B), the only warm colour. Quiet neutrals for secondary shapes: soft ink (#354544), muted grey-green (#556564), light surface (#F1F5F4) and pale line grey (#DCE5E3). Outlines and lettering in ink (#0F1A1A); no pure black, no other colours. No hatching, no cross-hatching, no paper texture, no grain, no gradients, no shading, no photorealism. Few details and plenty of white space. Light, warm, optimistic mood. Hand-lettered text is allowed. This series has a recurring robot with his own look (boxy head with antenna, segmented arms and legs); the figure rule above applies to the human figures only and does not change him. Captions are hand-lettered in the ink colour and underlined in the ink colour as well; the underline belongs to the lettering and is not a colour accent. Square composition.


IMAGE PROMPT ("Scheitern") — GOAL, NOT A CHECKLIST
==============================================================

This brief tells you what the picture is for. The staging is yours.

WHAT THE POST SAYS
In mid-September Christian declared one of his own experiments failed, in public:
two formats on LinkedIn, one did not carry, so there is only one now. A coach who
helps organisations experiment, admitting that an experiment of his own failed,
sounds like bad advertising. His answer: there is no learning journey without
failures, hiding one would make the whole journey dishonest, and the failed
experiment does not damage the brand, it adds to it. What did not work is what
makes the rest of the account believable, a status report included.

WHAT THE PICTURE SHOULD DO
Someone scrolling past should see a person showing a failure openly and calmly,
as a normal part of the way, and feel that this makes him more trustworthy, not
less. Relaxed and matter-of-fact, not a confession, not a disaster, not shame.

THE MOTIF DIRECTION
The person stands at a flipchart or wall that shows his journey as a row of
sheets or a hand-drawn path, and pins one sheet among the others where everyone
can see it: a sheet that plainly did not work out, a line that runs downhill or a
crossed-out shape. He looks at it with a small, relaxed smile. Steer away from:
gravestones and burials (the post of 17.09 already ended an experiment), red
crosses, alarm signs, broken things lying on the floor, crying or slumped
postures, speech bubbles full of text, screens and dashboards.

WHAT IS FIXED
The style guide above, including its palette: this is the first post in the new
colours, so Petrol (#008888) goes on the one thing the eye should land on first,
the failed sheet. Square composition, white background. One figure only, the
stick-figure person; the story is about Christian alone, so no robot and no other
person, not even faintly. No readable words, numbers or digits on the sheets. Any
hand-lettering is German, since the post is German. A hand-lettered caption at
the bottom in the ink colour, underlined in the ink colour, reading exactly:
"Auch das gehört zur Reise". Fetch two existing post PNGs from the same folder as
visual reference for hand and figures only: they still carry the old colours, so
take no colour from them.

WHAT IS YOURS
The staging: flipchart, wall or a path of sheets, how many sheets, where the
failed one sits among them. Keep it one clear moment with plenty of room around it.
