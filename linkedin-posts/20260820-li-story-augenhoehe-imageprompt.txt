IMAGE STYLE GUIDE ("Zander Flipchart" — use for every future post)
====================================================================

Hand-drawn flipchart illustration, as sketched live by a trainer with markers: clean black marker outlines on a plain white background, simple friendly shapes, hand-lettered text. Human figures are always drawn with a body — a round head, an oval torso, and thin arms and legs attached to that torso, never a head with limbs hanging directly off it — and the posture carries the expression. This applies to the human figures only; the robot of this series keeps his own look (boxy head with antenna, segmented arms and legs) and is not changed by it. Accents only in classic flipchart marker colors — yellow, orange, green, blue, red — applied as wide, slightly uneven marker strokes. No gradients, no photorealism, no dark shading, plenty of white space. Light, warm, optimistic mood. Square composition.


IMAGE PROMPT ("Kein Widerspruch") — GOAL, NOT A CHECKLIST
==========================================================

This brief is written differently on purpose. It does not describe the scene element by element. It tells you what the picture is for, and leaves the motif to you.

WHAT THE POST SAYS
The team was built flat on purpose: one team, the human and the agent both in it, each with a role, the permissions running through it, and the human deliberately inside rather than beside it. Then the post turns on itself. Among people, that flatness is work — it has to be earned and defended against ego, career interest and the fear of consequences, and whoever sets out to please the leader ends up pursuing a goal of their own. The agent has none of that, and so the word does not fit here: equal footing needs someone who has interests and brings them in. In this team there is exactly one will in the room, and it is the human's. The hierarchy has not disappeared. It is simply never put to the test. From here on the contradiction has to be produced deliberately, or the agent has to be set up to provoke it — while among people it comes for free, as long as they feel safe enough to say it out loud.

WHAT THE PICTURE SHOULD DO
Someone scrolling past should sense that a team in which nobody ever objects looks harmonious and proves nothing. The surface stays friendly; what is missing sits underneath it. Aim for a quiet unease inside a pleasant scene, not for gloom, a warning or a verdict. Avoid anything that makes the agent look sinister or obedient in a creepy way, and anything that makes the human look foolish: he is the one noticing this about his own team, and he says it about himself.

WHAT IS FIXED
The style guide above. Square composition, white background. The small friendly stick-figure robot that carries this series is the agent, and the stick-figure person is his human colleague. The team has exactly these two members — one human and one agent. Do not add further people to make it look like a team: colleagues who do not exist would make the picture say something untrue. Any hand-lettering in the picture is German, since the post is German. A hand-lettered caption at the bottom in black marker, underlined, reading exactly: "Kein Widerspruch". As always, fetch two existing post PNGs from the same folder as visual reference so the series keeps one look.

WHAT IS YOURS
The motif, the composition, and which single detail carries the message. Pick one clear idea and let it breathe, rather than assembling several.

TWO REQUESTS
If the goal above is unclear or you can read it in more than one way, say so in the pull request description instead of guessing.
And write one or two sentences into the pull request description explaining which idea you chose and why. That is the point of this experiment: this brief gives you a goal instead of a list, and we want to see what you do with the freedom.
