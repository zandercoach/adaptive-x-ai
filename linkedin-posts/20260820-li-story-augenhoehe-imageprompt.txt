IMAGE STYLE GUIDE ("Zander Flipchart" — use for every future post)
====================================================================

Hand-drawn flipchart illustration, as sketched live by a trainer with markers: clean black marker outlines on a plain white background, simple friendly shapes, hand-lettered text. Human figures are always drawn with a body — a round head, an oval torso, and thin arms and legs attached to that torso, never a head with limbs hanging directly off it — and the posture carries the expression. This applies to the human figures only; the robot of this series keeps his own look (boxy head with antenna, segmented arms and legs) and is not changed by it. Accents only in classic flipchart marker colors — yellow, orange, green, blue, red — applied as wide, slightly uneven marker strokes. No gradients, no photorealism, no dark shading, plenty of white space. Light, warm, optimistic mood. Square composition.


IMAGE PROMPT ("Im Team, nicht darüber") — GOAL, NOT A CHECKLIST
================================================================

This brief is written differently on purpose. It does not describe the scene element by element. It tells you what the picture is for, and leaves the motif to you.

WHAT THE POST SAYS
Setting up the collaboration with the agent needed a team, and the first proposal was to put all the bots in a group of their own. That was rejected: a team of only bots is a pen, not a team. So there is exactly one team, the human and the agent are both in it, each with a role, and the permissions run through it — who belongs is readable from the structure. The point behind it is the one that shows up in every team: does the person carrying the responsibility stand beside the team and watch, or inside it? Standing next to it is not membership. Everyone in the team contributes to the team's goal as best they can — and someone who is trying to please the leader instead is pursuing a different goal, which is exactly why the leader belongs inside.

WHAT THE PICTURE SHOULD DO
Someone scrolling past should see, before reading a word, that the human is *inside* the team rather than above or beside it — and should read that as natural and relaxed, not as a statement being made. The feeling to aim at is ordinary togetherness: two members, same level, no one presiding. If a second idea fits without crowding the first, the rejected alternative may echo somewhere — a group of only bots, which is a pen rather than a team. If it does not fit, drop it: the leader standing inside is the message.

WHAT IS FIXED
The style guide above. Square composition, white background. The small friendly stick-figure robot that carries this series is the agent, and the stick-figure person is his human colleague. The team has exactly these two members — one human and one agent. Do not add further people to make it look like a team: colleagues who do not exist would make the picture say something untrue. Any hand-lettering in the picture is German, since the post is German. A hand-lettered caption at the bottom in black marker, underlined, reading exactly: "Mittendrin". As always, fetch two existing post PNGs from the same folder as visual reference so the series keeps one look.

WHAT IS YOURS
The motif, the composition, and which single detail carries the message. Pick one clear idea and let it breathe, rather than assembling several.

And write one or two sentences into the pull request description explaining which idea you chose and why. That is the point of this experiment: this brief gives you a goal instead of a list, and we want to see what you do with the freedom.
