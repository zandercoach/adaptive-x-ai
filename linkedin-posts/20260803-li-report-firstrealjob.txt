Adaptive × AI — Morten's first real job

More from the lab notebook of my learning journey on adaptive-x-ai. One day after my marketing agent Morten went live as a queue watchdog, I gave him his first real task: turn my LinkedIn analytics into a proper statistics report as a PDF.

Getting there took three attempts, and I let him take all three. His first version leaned on a charting library that needs a browser; in the PDF it looked neither good nor right. For the second he drew the charts himself, and the pie slices came out bulgy and lopsided 😉 For the third he wrote his own little rendering program, and that one finally looked the part. Three different approaches, each triggered by nothing but my feedback: an agent iterates like a developer, if you let it. And what helped him most was naming the audience. "CEO-friendly" did more than any list of font sizes would have.

One moment went sideways. We had already established, his own conclusion included, that the route we wanted was closed. On the very next attempt he looped on it anyway, firing the same futile calls until I stopped him: work only from the numbers you already have. He knew better, and did it anyway. That boundary is now written into his brief.

Which brings me to the wall itself. There is no analytics API for a personal LinkedIn profile: the real ones cover company pages behind a partner review, and the one legal route to my own numbers exists only because EU regulation forced it into being. So I stopped treating the manual export as a stopgap and designed it as the process. Once a month I export by hand, Morten reminds me in his Friday mail, and he builds the report. Two minutes of my time beats weeks of fragile plumbing. A human in the loop can be the interface by design.

Full disclosure, as always: this is a learning report, not a how-to — what I tried, what looked wrong, where the wall actually stood. The full journal is public (link in the first comment).

Next up: the day Morten started opening his own pull requests, and I learned that a wrong image is really a wrong prompt. More to come, warts included.
