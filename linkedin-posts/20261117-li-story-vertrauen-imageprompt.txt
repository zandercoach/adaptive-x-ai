IMAGE STYLE GUIDE ("zander.coach flipchart style" — use for every future post)
==============================================================================

Hand-drawn flipchart illustration, as sketched live by a trainer with markers: clean near-black marker outlines on a plain white background, simple friendly shapes. Human figures are always drawn with a body — a round head, an oval torso, and single-line arms and legs attached to that torso, each ending in a small round hand or foot, never a head with limbs hanging directly off it — and the posture carries the expression. Each figure is about five heads tall: a large round head, a tall narrow oval torso about one and a half heads long, long straight legs. Faces are two dot eyes and a small curved smile, no nose, the head often turned slightly to one side; hair is a few loose marker strokes that tell the figures apart. Torsos are filled flat or left white. One even line weight throughout, like a medium marker at about half a percent of the image width, never thicker for emphasis. Colours only from this palette, applied as wide, slightly uneven marker strokes and flat fills. Petrol (#008888) is the eye-catcher: used sparingly, on the one or two things the eye should land on first. Supporting accents: deep petrol (#005F5F), deep sea (#06393A), pale petrol (#C7DBD8) and sand (#A98B5B), the only warm colour. Quiet neutrals for secondary shapes: soft ink (#354544), muted grey-green (#556564), light surface (#F1F5F4) and pale line grey (#DCE5E3). Outlines and lettering in ink (#0F1A1A); no pure black, no other colours. No hatching, no cross-hatching, no paper texture, no grain, no gradients, no shading, no photorealism. Few details and plenty of white space. Light, warm, optimistic mood. Hand-lettered text is allowed. This series has a recurring robot with his own look (boxy head with antenna, segmented arms and legs); the figure rule above applies to the human figures only and does not change him. Captions are hand-lettered in the ink colour and underlined in the ink colour as well; the underline belongs to the lettering and is not a colour accent. Square composition.


IMAGE PROMPT ("Vertrauen") — GOAL, NOT A CHECKLIST
==============================================================

This brief tells you what the picture is for. A motif direction is named below;
the staging is yours.

WHAT THE POST SAYS
In September Morten found a third instance of an inconsistent number, in a row
Christian had not named. He reported it and left it alone, where weeks earlier he
had acted on a finding himself. For Christian that was an agent following a rule
he did not have before, and his own trust growing; it still had to confirm itself.
In October a renamed picture arrived empty under "contents unchanged". Christian
believed it and approved, then caught it himself on the finishing pull and
restored it; since then file sizes are compared. Trust grows from moments like
September's, but it tempts you to assume reliability; checks are and stay
important.

WHAT THE PICTURE SHOULD DO
Someone scrolling past should see the good moment, a find handed over instead of
fixed on the quiet, and feel the small warmth of trust growing. The empty picture
of October stays in the text. Not a guilty robot, not a suspicious human, nothing
that reads as control.

THE MOTIF DIRECTION
The person stands beside the robot, the flipchart behind them. The robot holds out
the sticky note and the person's open hand reaches for it, so the hand-over is the
centre of the picture. On the flipchart sheet, a grid of scribbled rows with one
cell circled; the robot keeps his hands off the sheet. The person looks pleased
and attentive. Steer away from: warning signs, red crosses, magnifying glasses,
screens, speech bubbles full of text.

WHAT IS FIXED
The style guide above. Square composition, white background. Two figures of this
series and no invented third: the stick-figure person and the robot. The person
keeps the proportions the style guide gives: about five heads tall, a tall narrow
torso about one and a half heads long, long straight legs. The sheet and the note
carry scribbled lines only, with no readable words, numbers or digits. Petrol goes
on what the eye should land on first: the sticky note and the circled cell. Any
hand-lettering is German, since the post is German. A hand-lettered caption at the
bottom in the ink colour, underlined in the ink colour, reading exactly:
"Gemeldet, nicht angefasst". Fetch two existing post PNGs from the same folder as
visual reference so the series keeps one look.

WHAT IS YOURS
How the two lean in, how the note is held out, and which supporting palette
colours do the rest. Keep it one clear moment with plenty of room around it.
